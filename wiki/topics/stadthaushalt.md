# City budget (*Stadthaushalt*)

Munich's budget has two parts. The **results budget** (*Ergebnishaushalt*) records ordinary income and expenses on an accrual basis, including depreciation; the **financial budget** (*Finanzhaushalt*) records actual payments, including investments and loans. The city treasury (*Stadtkämmerei*) publishes the actual results per **product** (about 200 city services) as open data.

## Actual results 2024 (open data)

- Ordinary income **€8.96 bn**, ordinary expenses **€9.86 bn**, ordinary result **-€903.7 m**. In 2018 income and expenses were both about €7.4 bn.
- Largest departments by expenses: education and sports (*Referat für Bildung und Sport*) €2.57 bn, social department (*Sozialreferat*) €1.91 bn, treasury €1.20 bn (it books taxes and general allocations, and most of the city's income: €6.15 bn).
- Examples of net cost (expenses minus income) per resident, with 1,603,776 residents at the end of 2024: fire protection (*Brandschutz*) €210 m or €131 per resident, with expenses up 42 % since 2018; the city library (*Münchner Stadtbibliothek*) €52.6 m in expenses.
- Ask `city_budget` for any service ("Kita", "libraries", "Friedhöfe", "fire brigade"), a department ("Kulturreferat", "KVR"), or the whole city.

## Supplementary budget 2026 (*Nachtragshaushalt*)

Adopted by the full council on **30 September 2026** (Rathaus Umschau):

- Results budget: expenses up about €169 m to **€10.67 bn**, income up about €129 m to **€10.03 bn**; the deficit grows by €40 m to **€638 m** and is offset against the results reserve (*Ergebnisrücklage*). Main causes: higher ordinary expenses, depreciation and transfer payments.
- Financial budget: surplus from current operations **€223 m**, enough to cover planned debt repayment of about €143 m, so the budget meets the legal requirements.
- Investment payments cut by about €304 m to **€2.59 bn**; loans for investments reduced by **€460 m to €1.95 bn**.
- Treasurer Christoph Frey: the situation "remains tense"; total debt is expected to keep rising until 2029, and consolidation and a review of the city's tasks (*Aufgabenkritik*) continue.

## Caveats

- City-owned companies such as Stadtwerke München have their own accounts; only the city's payments to them appear in the product data.
- Some products are central functions (for example central personnel accounting), so department totals follow the city's product allocation rather than who delivers a service on the ground.
